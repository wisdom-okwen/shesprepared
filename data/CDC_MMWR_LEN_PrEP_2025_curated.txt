CDC MMWR: Clinical Recommendation for the Use of Injectable Lenacapavir as HIV Preexposure Prophylaxis — United States, 2025

Key Findings:
- Lenacapavir (LEN) is a new injectable PrEP option administered subcutaneously every 6 months.
- Efficacy in clinical trials:
  - PURPOSE 1 (cisgender women): 100% effective compared to no PrEP (IRR=0) and 100% compared to daily TDF/FTC (IRR=0).
  - PURPOSE 2 (primarily males and gender-diverse persons): 96% effective compared to no PrEP (IRR=0.04) and 89% effective compared to daily TDF/FTC (IRR=0.11).
- Both oral (daily TDF/FTC) and injectable (LEN or cabotegravir) PrEP are highly effective when taken as directed, with efficacy up to 99% in preventing HIV infection.
- LEN is strongly recommended by CDC for persons weighing ≥77 lbs (≥35 kg) at risk for HIV acquisition.
- Common side effects: Injection site reactions (pain, nodules, induration), mostly mild to moderate.
- LEN provides long-acting protection, potentially improving adherence compared to daily oral PrEP.

Source: Patel RR, et al. MMWR Morb Mortal Wkly Rep 2025;74:541–549. DOI: http://dx.doi.org/10.15585/mmwr.mm7435a1
